Understanding Loans in Canada

Borrowing money is a major financial decision. Learn how loans work, what factors affect borrowing costs, and what questions to consider before exploring lending options.

What Is A Loan?

A loan is an agreement where money is provided by one party to another with the expectation that it will be repaid over time.


Most loans involve repayment of the original amount borrowed plus interest and may include additional fees depending on the lender and agreement.

Common Types Of Loans

Personal Loans

Personal loans are commonly used for expenses such as major purchases, projects, or consolidating existing obligations.

Auto Loans

Auto loans are designed specifically for financing vehicle purchases and typically use the vehicle as security.

Lines Of Credit

Lines of credit allow borrowers to access funds up to an approved limit and repay what they use.

Secured Loans

Secured loans involve an asset being used as security for the borrowing agreement.

Understanding Interest Rates

Interest is the cost associated with borrowing money. A lender may charge interest based on factors such as the loan agreement, market conditions, and borrower information.


A lower interest rate generally means less interest paid over time, while a higher rate may increase the total cost of borrowing.

Important Loan Terms To Understand

Principal

The original amount of money borrowed before interest and fees.

Interest Rate

The percentage charged for borrowing money.

Loan Term

The length of time scheduled for repayment.

Monthly Payment

The scheduled amount paid toward the loan.

Questions To Consider Before Borrowing

  • Why am I borrowing money?
  • How much will the loan cost over time?
  • Can the repayment schedule fit my financial situation?
  • Are there additional fees or conditions?
  • Have I compared available options?

How Loans Can Affect Credit

Loan activity may become part of a person's credit history. Factors such as payment history, account management, and overall credit behaviour can influence how credit information is viewed.


Understanding loan agreements before borrowing can help people make more informed financial decisions.
